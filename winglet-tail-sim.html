Winglet & Tail Sizing — Fuel Burn Simulator

Change the winglet height and the horizontal and vertical tail areas. The model recomputes the drag build-up, cruise lift-to-drag ratio and block fuel for a fixed 3,000 km mission, and checks the two constraints that set tail size in practice: longitudinal stability and engine-out yaw control.
Disclaimer: This is a simplified teaching model, not an engineering tool. It uses textbook approximations and typical values for a generic narrow-body aircraft, and it leaves out climb, descent, compressibility and structural effects. Use it to compare trends between configurations. The numbers it gives are not accurate predictions for any real aircraft.

Model & assumptions

Drag polar

C_D = C_D0 + C_L,w² / (π e AR_eff) + C_D,trim

Parasitic drag of each surface: C_f · FF · S_wet / S_ref, with C_f = 0.0028, FF = 1.30, S_wet ≈ 2.05 × planform area. Fuselage, wing and nacelles together: 158 counts. Oswald factor e = 0.80.

Winglet effect

AR_eff = AR · (1 + 1.9 h / b)

Raymer's approximation for the effective aspect-ratio gain from a vertical winglet of height h. Penalties: added wetted area (mean winglet chord 0.8 m) and 110 kg per metre of height per side for the winglet and wing reinforcement.

Trim (horizontal tail)

L_t = (M_ac + W·d) / (l_t + d) C_L,t = L_t / (η q S_h)

Moment balance about the CG with C_m,ac = −0.08 and the CG 0.12 MAC aft of the wing-body aerodynamic centre. The wing carries W − L_t. A smaller tail must work at a higher C_L,t, which adds tail induced drag (AR_t = 5).

Static margin

h_n = 0.18 + 0.263 · V_h , V_h = S_h l_h / (S c̄) SM = h_n − h_cg

η·(a_t/a)·(1 − dε/dα) = 0.263, l_h = 17 m, c̄ = 4.19 m. Minimum acceptable SM taken as 5% MAC.

Engine-out yaw control

S_v,req = T · y_e / (q_mc · C_Y,max · l_v)

One engine at T = 110 kN, 5.75 m off the centreline, held at V_mc = 65 m/s at sea level with C_Y,max = 0.75 and l_v = 16 m. Gives S_v,req ≈ 20.4 m².

Fuel (Breguet)

W_i / W_f = exp( R · c · g / (V · L/D) )

V = 231 m/s, c = 1.6×10⁻⁵ kg/(N·s). L/D evaluated at mid-mission weight (iterated). Landing weight 62.2 t at baseline (OEW + 180 pax × 95 kg + reserves), plus or minus the structural weight of the changes. Fuel density 0.80 kg/L.

What this model leaves out

Climb and descent segments, wing-bending relief from winglets, compressibility drag, tail interference drag, gust loads and certification limits on span. Results are for comparing trends between configurations, not for predicting a specific aircraft.
